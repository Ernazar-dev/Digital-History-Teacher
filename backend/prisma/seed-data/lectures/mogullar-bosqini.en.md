## 1. Sources and Formation of the Mongol State

### Sources

The main sources on the conquest are the works of Ibn al-Asir "Al-Kamil fit-tarih," Shahabiddin an-Nasawi "Siyrat as-sultan Jalaliddin Mankburni," Minhojiddin Juzjani "Tabaqati Nasiri," Atamalik Juvayni "Tarikh-i Jahangusho," Rashiduddin "Jame at-Tawarih." The topic has been studied by V.V. Barthold, I.P. Petrushevsky, Z.M. Bunyodov, and many foreign scientists. Since most sources belong to the pen of Muslim authors who suffered from the invasion, or historians in the service of the Mongols, they must be compared and evaluated.

### Temuchin — Genghis Khan

The Mongols are first mentioned in the Chinese chronicle of the Tang dynasty under the name "menu"; they were formed as a people in the 13th century. After the Mongol leader Esugay-Bagatur was killed by the Tatars, his son Temuchin (1155-1227), a strong, enterprising, and cunning commander, united the scattered tribes: in 1186, he subjugated the Markites, then the Tatars, and in 1203, he defeated the Kereit khan Ung-khan. In 1206, at the general kurultai of the Mongols, Temuchin was proclaimed supreme khan and received the title "Genghis Khan" ("strong," "great"). The state was called the Unified Mongol Ulus (Great Mongol State), the capital was Karakorum; all of Mongolia was divided into 95 military-administrative units (thousands).

### Yasaka Laws

The state relied on a yarlyk (order), a yasak (law), and a bilig (instruction). The "Yasak" was a collection of strict laws reflecting the patriarchal military system, according to the main provisions of which the khan swore to strictly observe the "Yasak," otherwise he could be imprisoned for life; everyone was granted religious freedom, and the clergy were exempted from taxes; the death penalty was widely used as a punishment; there was no mercy for conquered peoples.

Genghis Khan increased his personal guard (keshiktan) to 9,000 men; In 1204, the Uyghur alphabet was adopted for the Mongolian script. In 1209, the Tanguts were conquered, in 1211, the Uyghurs, and in 1215, Northern China (including Beijing). The Mongols extensively utilized the experience of Uyghur and Muslim merchants, often using them as spies.

## 2. Relations between Genghis Khan and Muhammad Khwarazmshah

After the conquest of China, the state of Genghis Khan bordered its strongest neighbor in the west, the state of the Khwarazmshahs and Anushtagins (1097-1231). Relations between the two states gradually escalated.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1215 | Khwarazmshah's ambassador, Bahauddin Razi, was received by Genghis Khan in Beijing |
| 1215/1217 | In the Turgay steppe, the Khwarazmshah's army encountered the army of Jochi |
| 1217 | Khwarazmshah's unsuccessful campaign against the Baghdad Caliphate |
| 1218 (spring) | The Mongol ambassadors led by Mahmud Yalavach were received in Bukhara |
| 1218 | The tragedy in Otrar — a caravan of 450 merchants was looted and its members were killed |
| 1219 (September) | The Mongol invasion began |

In Chingiz Khan's letter, it is said: "I am the ruler of the East, you are the ruler of the West! "Let there be a firm agreement between us on friendship and peace..." In his next letter, he called the Khwarazmshah his "most precious son," which in Eastern etiquette meant submission or patronage.

### The tragedy of Otrar

In 1218, Genghis Khan sent a caravan of 450 Muslim merchants and 500 camels. Inal Khan (Gair Khan), the ruler of Otrar and a relative of Turkan-Khatun, accused them of espionage, exterminated them, and plundered their livestock. Genghis Khan sent an ambassador, Ibn Kafraj Bughra, to demand that Inal Khan be handed over. Although Prince Jalal ad-Din offered to extradite Inal Khan to prevent war, the Sultan refused and ordered the ambassador to be killed. Textbook Conclusion: Chingiz Khan officially named the Khwarazmshah as the culprit of the war and called himself a "noble liberator"; in reality, he was thoroughly preparing for the war. So, the cause of the war was both sides. In 1218, Jebe defeated the Kuchluk state in Semirechye and Kashgar and seized lands as far as the Talas Valley.

## 3. Weaknesses of the Khwarazmshah state

The state of the Khwarazmshahs was a great state in the Muslim world, whose army reached 400 thousand people, which was twice as many as the Mongols. However, the state's internal weakness led to its downfall. In fact, Turkan-hatun (the Sultan's mother) was the first person to rely on the Qipchaq commanders; her decree prevailed over the Sultan's, and she placed her grandson Ozlak-shah on the throne and removed Jaloliddin. The army was mainly mercenary, poorly disciplined, and intended for conquest and plunder.

Taxes were collected arbitrarily: in 1218, kharaj was collected three times at a time; In 1206 and 1212, popular uprisings occurred in Bukhara and Samarkand. The Sultan's campaign against the Caliph, the proclamation of the Termez Sheikh Alamulk as Caliph, and the execution of Majd al-Din Baghdadi increased the dissatisfaction of the ulema. Furthermore, the sultan did not trust his commanders. Together, these factors weakened the state from within.

## 4. The Mongol invasion of Maveraunnahr

### Military Council in Urgench

When the invasion began, the sultan convened a council in Urgench. Shahabuddin al-Khivaki proposed gathering all troops on the banks of the Syr Darya and delivering a decisive blow right at the border. Jalaluddin suggested letting the enemy in and destroying them, knowing the conditions of the area. But under pressure from the sultan and his mother, a defensive method was chosen: the army was scattered across about 450 cities and fortresses. It was a catastrophic mistake.

### Invasion progress

In 1219, Genghis Khan marched out with an army of about 200 thousand and divided it into four parts at Otrar. Chigatai and Oktay were sent to the siege of Otrar, Jochi to Jend, Yangikent, and Sygnak in the lower reaches of the Syr Darya, Ulak Noyon (5 thousand) to Khujand and Banakat; Genghis Khan himself (together with Jebe and Subutoy) marched on Bukhara and Samarkand.

| City | Defense | Fate |
|---|---|---|
| Otrar | Inal Khan resisted for 6 months (5 months in the city, 1 month in the fortress) | Karacha was arrested for treason against the Hajib; Inalkhon was killed, having poured silver into his ears and eyes |
| Bukhara | It was taken on February 10, 1220; 400 men under the leadership of Kuk Khan resisted in the arch for 12 days | 30,000 people were killed, and the city was burned |
| Samarkand | 110 thousand warriors; the ruler Tugay Khan betrayed; 1000 people fought in the cathedral mosque | It was taken on March 17, 1220; only a quarter of the population remained alive; 30 thousand artisans have been allocated |
| Khujand | Timur-Melik defended himself on the island of Syr Darya, then with 70 ships, he forced his way across the river | Timur-Melik joined Jalaluddin |
| Termez | Hokim Fakhriddin defended himself for 11 days | The population has been exterminated |
| Gurganj (Urgench) | 90 thousand troops; siege for 7 months (1221); battle for every street | The dam of the Amu Darya has been destroyed, the city has been flooded; More than 100,000 artisans were exiled to Mongolia |

Rashid al-Din describes the actions of Genghis Khan, who captured Bukhara, as follows: the Mongols loaded the Quran trunks into the stables, while the ulema and sheikhs were forced to guard the horses in the stables. Qadi Badriddin and Imam Rukniddin, unable to endure this disgrace, rushed at the enemy and perished.

### The Fate of Sultan Muhammad

After capturing Samarkand, the sultan fled south-70 loads of gold drowned in the water while crossing the Amu Darya. An elite 20-thousand-strong army led by Jebe and Subedei followed him closely. Before his death, the sultan proclaimed Jalaluddin as the heir to the throne and said: "Only my son Jalaluddin can take revenge for me." He died in December 1220 on Ashuradi Island in the Caspian Sea. Turkan Khatun surrendered after a 4-month siege of the Mazandaran fortress and died in captivity in Karakorum in 1233.

### Najmuddin Kubro and the Consequences of Invasion

During the defense of Gurganj, the founder of the Kubravi order, 76-year-old Najmiddin Kubra (1145-1221), fought with his disciples under the slogan "Either the Motherland, or a glorious death" and died a heroic death. In three years, the great Khwarazmshah state was conquered. Nisa (70 thousand people were killed), Marv, Balkh, Nishapur, and Bamiyan were destroyed. For a long time, cities such as Otrar and Sygnak were transformed into deserts. As Ibn al-Asir wrote: "They spared no one... The waves of this disaster spread in different directions, and its tragedy became common.

## 5. Jaloliddin Manguberdi — Hero of the Motherland

Jalaluddin Manguberdi (al-Nasawi — Mankburni, "nosed") fought against the Mongols for 11 years, winning 13 out of 14 battles. The lands bordering Ghazni, Bamiyan, Gur, and India were granted to him as his property.

### Main battles

| Date | Battle | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Beginning 1221 | Near Nisa — 300 warriors against 700 Mongols | Victory; a blow to the myth of the "invincible Mongols" |
| 1221 | Under Kandahar, for the first time in history, infantry archers were stationed against the horsemen | Victory in a three-day battle |
| 1221 | Near the Valiyon fortress | More than 1,000 Mongols were killed |
| 1221 (autumn) | Battle of Parvona — Shiki against the Hutuhu army | Complete victory; Uprisings began in Marv, Herat, and Bukhara |
| November 25, 1221 | The three-day battle at the Sindh River against Genghis Khan himself | Injury; Jalaluddin jumped into the river on his horse and swam to the other side |
| September 5, 1227 | The secret against the Noyon at Isfahan | Victory; the Mongols are driven out of Iran |
| August 10, 1230 | Against the alliance of the Sultan of Konya and other rulers | Defeat |

After Parwan's victory, disagreements arose between the commanders Amin Malik and Sayfiddin Ugrak regarding the division of the spoils, and a portion of the army split, resulting in the defeat at the Battle of Sind. Chingiz Khan, admiring Jalaluddin's bravery on the banks of the Sindh, told his sons: "A father should have only such a son!" and forbade them from pursuing him.

### India and the Iran-Caucasus struggle

Jalaluddin conquered the coastal lands of Sindh in India; His authority was recognized by the Sultan of Delhi, Eltutmish. From 1224, he created a large state, capturing Kerman, Persia, Iraq, Azerbaijan (1225), and Georgia (1226, 1228). He freed Tabriz from the three-year tax and punished the corrupt vizier al-Jandi. Jalaluddin called upon neighboring Muslim rulers to unite against the Mongols, but they did not join the alliance, fearing its strengthening; The Baghdad Caliphate and the Ismailis even became close to the Mongols. In August 1231, when he was suddenly attacked by the Mongols, Jalaluddin was killed by a Kurd in a mountain village near Mayafarikin.

The ruler of Damascus, al-Malik al-Ashraf, upon hearing of his death, said: "His death means the invasion of the Mongols into the Islamic world. Now there is no one who can be a wall between us and Gog and Magog like Khwarazmshah

### Perpetuation of memory

In 1998, the 800th anniversary of Jaloliddin Manguberdi was widely celebrated by a resolution of the Cabinet of Ministers; In 1999, a majestic monument to him was erected in Urgench, and on August 30, 2000, the Order of Jaloliddin Manguberdi was established

## 6. Political and socio-economic life in the Chigatai Ulus

### Division into uluses

Genghis Khan divided the conquered lands among his sons into uluses.

| Son | Ulus |
|---|---|
| Jochi | From Southern Siberia to Khorezm and Derbent |
| Chigatai | From the Southern Altai to the Amu Darya and Sindh (Movarounnahr) |
| Consolidated | China and Mongolia (successor to the Khaganate) |
| Tuli | Iran and Khorasan |

The Khwarazmian merchant Mahmud Yalavach (residence in Khujand) was appointed governor of Transoxiana. At his disposal were tax collectors-baskaks, darughas-and military detachments.

### Taxes and obligations

| Tax | Content |
|---|---|
| Large | Land tax — 1/10 of the harvest |
| Thick | Livestock tax — 1 head for every 100 head (since 1235) |
| Scoundrel | Food tax — a two-year-old sheep from each herd, one mare from every 1000 horses |
| Mark | Craftsmen's Tax |

Owners of a share (gold, silver, or wooden board) could demand horses, fodder, and food products from the population free of charge. The expenses for the postal stations-the pits-lay on the shoulders of the population. The situation of artisans was difficult: they worked in "enterprises" at a slave level. From 1232, monetary circulation ceased in Samarkand, and trade shifted to the level of exchange. The gift of land and property was now called a suyurgal.

### Reforms

A number of reforms were carried out to combat disorder in the tax and management systems. Munkakhan (1251-1259) abolished the old payza and yarlyk, established the number of horses on pits, and prohibited excessive demands on the population. Masud-bek (governor in 1238-1289) in 1271 carried out a monetary reform: pure silver coins of equal weight were minted in 16 cities and provinces; In 1273, he led the restoration of plundered Bukhara. During the reign of Duva Khan (1291-1306), the city of Andijan was founded. Kebek Khan (1318-1326) moved to Maveraunnahr and built a residence near Nasaf (later the city of Qarshi); he carried out administrative (district and provincial) and financial reforms, minted silver dinars (approximately 8 g) and dirhams (1 dinar = 6 dirhams). Tarmashirin (1326-1334) made Islam the official religion of the Chigatai Ulus; he was killed in a rebellion by the nomadic nobility.

### Dissolution of the ulus

In the 1840s, Moghulistan (Duglat Emirate) emerged in Semirechye. Although Kazan Khan (1343-1346) tried to strengthen central power, he was killed by Emir Kazagan in 1346. Movarounnahr was divided into semi-independent parts: Kesh was ruled by Haji Barlas, Khujand by Bayazid Jalair, Balkh by Emir Husayn, and Bukhara by the Sadrids. It was precisely this political fragmentation that created the historical foundation for the creation of a unified state under Amir Temur.

## 7. Mahmud Tarabi's Rebellion

In 1238, an uprising against the oppression of the Mongols and local nobility broke out in the village of Tarob, located 3 farsakhs from Bukhara, led by the craftsman Mahmud Tarobi. He called upon the people: "Whoever can, take up weapons-swords, bows, and clubs." The rebels occupied Bukhara; the Bukharan scholar Shamsiddin Mahbubi joined them. In the battle on the Karmana road, 10 thousand Mongols were killed, but Mahmud Tarabi and Shamsiddin Mahbubi perished. The new leaders (the Tarabi brothers) were inexperienced; a week later, a new Mongol army suppressed the uprising, killing more than 21,000 people on both sides.

The uprising showed that the people are always ready to fight for the freedom of the motherland. After this, the Mongols attempted to regulate taxes, and Mahmud Yalavach was removed from his post and replaced by his son Masudbek.

## 8. Cultural life in the second half of the 13th and first half of the 14th centuries

### Architecture

With the revival of the economy and the transition of the Mongols to a sedentary lifestyle, construction resumed; ganch carving, engraving, and architectural calligraphy developed once again. Monuments from this period include the mausoleum of Sayfiddin Boharzi in Bukhara (highly valued by Ibn Battuta), the Madrasas of Masudiyya and Khaniya, the mausoleum of Qusam ibn Abbas in Samarkand, the mausoleums of Najmiddin Kubro and Turabekhonim in Urgench, and the minaret (its surviving part is 62 meters high).

### Literature and historiography

The Mongol invasion dealt a great blow to the centers of science; cultural centers returned to Maveraunnahr and Khurasan only at the end of the 14th and beginning of the 15th centuries. During this period, Jalaluddin Rumi ("Masnaviyi Ma'navi"), Saadi Shirazi ("Gulistan," "Buston"), and Amir Khusrav Dehlavi ("Khamsa"), whose ancestors were from Kesh, became famous in Persian-Tajik literature. Historical works such as "Tabaqati Nasiri," "Tarihi Jahangusho," "Jame' al-Tawarih," and an-Nasawi's work on Jalaluddin were created. In Turkic literature, the Khorezmian philosopher-poet and invincible fighter Pahlavan Mahmud (1247-1326) praised human intellect and moral purity in his rubais and distributed gifts to orphans; Nasiruddin Rabghuzi wrote the work "Kissasi Rabghuzi" (72 parts) between 1309 and 1310.

### Bahauddin Naqshband

The founder of the Naqshbandi order, Bahauddin Naqshband (1318–1389), advocated for honest labor. His motto "Dil ba yoru, dast ba kor" ("The heart is with Allah, but the hands are in labor") became widespread among the people. During the years of independence, a memorial complex was erected on the site of his grave near Bukhara.

## 9. Consequences of the Mongol invasion and evaluations in historiography

### Political and socio-economic consequences

The Mongol invasion was an event that left a deep mark on the history of Maveraunnahr and Khurasan. Politically, the state of the Khwarazmshahs was liquidated, and the region was incorporated into the Mongol Empire and subordinated to the Chagatai Ulus. Instead of local rulers, the rule of Mongol governors, baskas, and darughas was introduced. The most severe consequences in socio-economic life were the destruction of cities and the death or capture of the majority of the population. Prosperous cities such as Bukhara, Samarkand, Gurganj, Marv, Balkh, and Nishapur suffered great losses, and many artisans were exiled to Mongolia. Irrigation systems were disrupted, which negatively affected agriculture. The security of trade routes was disrupted, money circulation was disrupted in some places, and taxes deteriorated.

### Cultural consequences

The conquest also dealt a great blow to the centers of science and culture: libraries were destroyed, and many scientists, poets, and theologians perished or were forced to leave for other countries. Despite this, the people sought to preserve their cultural heritage. The teachings of Bahauddin Naqshband, the work of Pahlavan Mahmud, and new historiographical works show that cultural life did not cease entirely. Over time, the Mongol rulers adopted a sedentary lifestyle, embraced Islam, and fell under the influence of local culture. This process intensified particularly during the reign of Tarmashirin and subsequent years.

### Evaluations in historiography

The Mongol invasion and its consequences have been evaluated differently by historians. While some researchers evaluate the invasion as a tragedy that delayed the region's development for a long time, others also note the role of the Mongol Empire in expanding trade and cultural ties between East and West. Along with the severe consequences of the conquest, the textbook emphasizes the courageous resistance of the people and the heroism of figures such as Jaloliddin Manguberdi, Temur Malik, Najmiddin Kubro, and Mahmud Tarabi. When evaluating historical events, it is important to consider the social status and political views of the author of sources, avoiding one-sided conclusions.

It should be noted that political fragmentation in the mid-14th century necessitated the creation of a centralized state led by Amir Temur. In this sense, the Mongol period in the history of Uzbekistan's statehood, along with its crisis, is assessed as a stage that created historical conditions for the emergence of a new, strong, centralized state.

## Conclusion

At the beginning of the 13th century, the Mongol state united by Genghis Khan transformed into a massive military force in a short period of time. The internal weakness of the Khwarazmshah state-dual power between Turkan-Khatun and the Sultan, a mercenary army, heavy taxes, conflicts with the ulema, and a mistake in defensive strategy-enabled the Mongols to seize the entire country in three years. The defenders of Otrar, Bukhara, Samarkand, Khujand, Gurganj, Najmiddin Kubro, Temur Malik, and especially Jaloliddin Manguberdi became vivid symbols of our people's patriotism. The conquest dealt a heavy blow to agricultural oases, cities, and culture; The uprising of Mahmud Tarabi showed that the people are not afraid of the struggle for freedom. At the end of the 13th and 14th centuries, as a result of the reforms of Masud-bek and Kebek-khan, the economy and culture were gradually restored, but the Chagatai Ulus ended in political fragmentation.

## Key concepts

- **Kurultai** — the highest council of the Mongol nobility.
- **Yasak** — Collection of the Laws of Genghis Khan.
- **Ulus** — The territory and population granted to the sons of Genghis Khan.
- **Naib, baskak, daruga** — the governor, tax collector, and military administrator in the Mongol administration.
- **Kalon, kapchur, shulen, tamga** — taxes in the Mongol era.
- **Payza** — a board that grants the right to demand payments from the population.
- **Yom** — postal communication station.
- **Suyurgal** — land ownership gifted during the Mongol era.
