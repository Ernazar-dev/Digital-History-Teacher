# The period of the Timurid reign. Social, political, economic, and cultural life

## 1. The political situation after the death of Amir Temur

During the campaign against China, Amir Temur died in the city of Otrar on February 18, 1405. He appointed his grandson, Pirmuhammad Jahangir, as his successor, but in a short period of time, this decision effectively lost its force. The empire, which united twenty-seven countries, was not internally strong: it did not have a single economic space, a single management tradition, or a common legal framework, and relied mainly on the personal power, military might, and authority of the ruler. Amir Temur, knowing the situation, appointed his sons and grandchildren as governors of the regions and divided the lands into suyurgals. Although this measure facilitated state administration, it created a foundation for the growth of centrifugal forces after the ruler's death. According to Fasih Hawafi, Timur left behind 36 sons and grandchildren, each claiming the throne.

The development of events showed that in conditions where the order of succession was not clearly defined, an open struggle between dynasty members was inevitable. On March 18, 1405, Miranshah's son Khalil Sultan captured Samarkand and declared himself the ruler of Maveraunnahr. His rule caused dissatisfaction among the population and emirs due to the embezzlement of the treasury, the interference of his wife Shodimulk in politics, and the insult of the influential Sheikh Muhammad Porso. In 1407, the heir to the throne appointed by Timur, Pirmuhammad, was killed by his own minister, and in 1408, Miranshah perished in a battle with Kara-Yusuf. Thus, the western part of the empire was effectively out of control.

In 1409, the ruler of Fergana, Khudaydad, captured Khalil Sultan, after which the ruler of Khurasan, Shah Rukh, crossed the Amu Darya and captured Samarkand. Shah Rukh pardoned Khalil Sultan and granted him the right to rule Ray. Thus, Shah Rukh Mirza was recognized as the supreme ruler of the Timurid state, and the kingdom entered a period of relative stability that lasted for nearly forty years. Historians evaluate this period as a complex period of cultural upsurge amidst rising political fragmentation.

## 2. The reign of Shah Rukh Mirza (1409-1447)

### Domestic Policy and Creativity

Shah Rukh (1377-1447) was the fourth son of Amir Temur and had been the ruler of Khorasan since 1397. In sources, he is described as "khagani said," meaning "happy khagan." Contemporaries describe him as an educated, pious, and just ruler. The capital of the empire was moved to Herat, and Mawarannahr was given to his eldest son, Ulugh Beg. Shohruh's policy was aimed not at expanding territories through wars, but at restoring destroyed cities, revitalizing economic life, and establishing diplomatic relations.

The city of Marv, which had not fully recovered from the Mongol invasion, was restored in 1410: a new canal was dug, and a mosque, market, and madrasah were built. A madrasah and a khanaka, a central bazaar, and the Baqi Safed palace were built in Herat in 1411. Most of the structures were built according to the designs of the master architects, the brothers Qavamiddin and Ghiyathiddin. Shah Rukh's wife, Gavkharshad Begum, built a large madrasah and a mausoleum in Herat, and a famous mosque in Mashhad. A large library was established in Herat, which became an important center for scholars. In 1429, Shah Rukh defeated the Emir of the Karakoyunlu, Iskandar, and fortified the western borders, dividing the regions among his sons and grandchildren as suyurgals.

### Foreign policy

During the reign of Shah Rukh, the international relations of the Timurid state expanded particularly. These connections are extensively covered in historical sources, particularly in the diaries of Ghiyath al-Din Naqqosh and Abdurazzak Samarqandi.

| Country | Content of connections |
|---|---|
| China | Chinese ambassadors arrived in 1409, 1412, and 1417; In 1419, Shah Rukh sent an embassy consisting of 530 people. This time, G'iyosiddin Naqqosh left a record of his diary |
| India | In 1442-1444, Abdurazzak Samarkandi visited the state of Vijayanagar as an ambassador |
| Egypt | Relations were established from 1429; Between 1444 and 1445, the "Kisva of Shah Rukh" was sent to the Kaaba |
| Ottomans | Friendly relations have been preserved |

Shah Rukh died in Rai Province on March 12, 1447, during a campaign against his rebellious grandson. His sons were also known as patrons of culture. Boysungur Mirza (1397-1433) organized a "Kitabkhana" in Herat and gathered forty calligraphers and artists; the famous copy of "Shahnameh" was copied here. By order of Ibrahim Sultan, Sharafuddin Ali Yazdi wrote the "Zafarname."

## 3. Mirzo Ulugbek — ruler and scholar

### Life and reign

Muhammad Taragay Ulugbek (1394-1449) was the eldest son of Shah Rukh and was born on March 22, 1394, in the city of Sultaniya. She spent her childhood under the care of her grandmother, Saroymulk Khanum, and her mother, Gavkharshadbegim, in the palace of Amir Temur. For marrying a woman from the Chingizid dynasty, he received the title of "Kuragon" (Khan's fiancé). In 1409, fifteen-year-old Ulugh Beg was appointed governor of Maveraunnahr, and his father became Emir Shahmalik. From 1411, he ruled the country independently, and this period lasted until 1449, nearly forty years.

During the reign of Ulugh Beg, the borders of Transoxiana were strengthened: Khwarazm was captured in 1413, Fergana in 1414-1415, and Kashgar in 1416. The campaign against Mongolia in 1425 ended successfully, and the campaign against Dashti Qipchaq in 1427 ended in defeat. In 1428, Ulugbek carried out a monetary reform and regulated trade, reducing some taxes. At the same time, his reign faced difficulties in finding a compromise with some layers of religious clergy and military emirs.

### Tragic ending

After the death of Shah Rukh, the struggle for the throne between the Timurids flared up again. In 1448, Ulugh Beg and his son Abdullatif defeated Alauddavla in the Battle of Tarnob, but the fact that the victory was associated not with the name of Abdullatif, but with the name of another son, Abdulaziz, turned him against his father. In October 1449, in a battle near the village of Damascus near Samarkand, Ulugh Beg was defeated, and the gates of Samarkand were closed to him. He renounced the throne and asked for permission to perform the Hajj, but was killed on October 27, 1449, by order of his son. Abdullatif, nicknamed the "patricide," died on May 8, 1450, as a result of an assassination attempt.

### Scientific and educational activities

The place of Ulugbek in history is determined, first and foremost, by his activities as a patron of science and a scholar. He built madrasas in Bukhara (1417), Samarkand (1420), and Gijduvan (1433). A hadith is inscribed on the portal of the Bukhara madrasah: "The pursuit of knowledge is obligatory for every Muslim man and woman." More than a hundred scholars gathered in Samarkand, among whom Qozizoda Rumi, Ghiyath al-Din Jamshid al-Kashi, and Ali Qushji hold a special place.

In 1424-1429, an observatory was built on the banks of the Obirahmat River. The building was a three-story structure with a circumference of about 47 meters and a height of 31 meters, inside which the largest sextant in the East was installed. The observatory's library holds about fifteen thousand volumes. The results of long-term observations were collected in the work "Zij-i Jadid al-Kuragani," which was completed in 1437; in it, the positions of 1018 stars were determined, and the coordinates of 683 geographical points were determined. Ulugh Beg's other works, "Tarikh-i Arba' Ulus" and treatises on music, are also of scientific importance.

Ulugh Beg's "Zij" gained wide recognition in Europe: it was studied in Oxford from 1643, in the work of Yan Geveliy published in 1693, he was depicted among the most famous astronomers, and one of the craters on the Moon was named after him. Voltaire called the scientific center in Samarkand the "Ulugbek Academy"; it is evaluated as the second academy after the Ma'mun Academy. In 1994, the 600th anniversary of Ulugbek's birth was celebrated by decision of UNESCO. Alisher Navoi wrote the following about him:

### Sultan Ulugbek of the Timur Khan lineage

> That the world has not seen a sultan like him.

## 4. Movarounnahr and Khorasan in the second half of the 15th century

With the death of Ulugh Beg, the political situation in the Timurid state became tense again. Abu Said Mirza (1451-1469) conquered Samarkand with the help of the Dasht-i Qipchaq khan Abulkhair Khan, captured Herat in 1457, and briefly united the two parts of the empire. However, during his reign, massacres became more frequent, and culture did not rise to the level of Ulugh Beg. In 1469, Abusaid was killed in a battle with the Akkoyunlu.

After that, the state split into two parts. Sultan Husayn Bayqara (1469-1506) ruled in Khorasan, while Sultan Ahmad (1469-1494) and his successors ruled in Transoxiana. The sluggishness, drunkenness, and internal strife of the rulers of Transoxiana intensified the fragmentation: in Hisar, Khusravshah, and in Tashkent and Mongolia, Yunus Khan and Mahmud Khan conducted themselves independently. In such a difficult situation, the influential Sheikh Khoja Ubaydullah Ahror (1404-1490) repeatedly acted as a mediator in ending the internecine wars between the rulers and called upon the people for peace.

Due to internal weakness, Muhammad Shaybani Khan, who arrived from Dasht-i Qipchaq in 1500-1506, conquered Maveraunnahr, then Khwarazm, and in 1507, by conquering Khorasan, put an end to the Timurid Empire.

## 5. Sultan Husayn Bayqara and the Cultural Environment of Herat

Sultan Husayn Bayqara (1438-1506) was the grandson of Umarshaykh Mirza and studied at school with Alisher Navoi. After a long struggle, he captured Herat on March 24, 1469, and ruled there for nearly forty years. During his reign, Herat became a major center of science and culture in the Muslim East. According to Khandamir, about forty large structures were built in Khurasan.

Husayn Bayqara issued a decree supporting the use of the Turkic language in writing, and himself, under the pseudonym "Husayni," created a divan in the Turkic language. In his "Risala," he proudly mentions that the number of poets in his kingdom reached a thousand. It is also noted that there were no international conflicts in his time, and Khurasan lived in peace and tranquility for many years.

However, starting from the 90s of the 15th century, the rebellions of the sons, palace feuds, and the sultan's passion for entertainment complicated the situation. Husayn Bayqara died on May 6, 1506, during a campaign against Shaybani Khan, and his heirs could not offer resistance. This phenomenon showed that cultural upsurge is insufficient for the preservation of a state if it is not combined with political and military stability.

## 6. Alisher Navoi — thinker and statesman

### Life path

Mir Nizomiddin Alisher Navoi was born on February 9, 1441, in Herat. His father, Ghiyath al-Din Muhammad, was a respected official at the Timurid court. Alisher fell in love with poetry from childhood, and at the age of 15, he became known as a bilingual poet who wrote in two languages-Turkic and Persian. He received his education in Herat, Mashhad, and Samarkand.

### As a statesman

Navoi held several high positions in the civil service: in 1469-1472, he was a seal, in 1472-1476, he was a minister (with the title of "amiri kabir"), and in 1487-1489, he was the ruler of Astrobod. From 1489, he renounced all official positions and devoted himself to creativity; Sultan Husayn bestowed upon him the title "Mukarrabi Hazrati Sultoni" — "The Closest to the Sultan." Navoi was a statesman who fought for justice: "As much as I could, I broke the blade of oppression and applied vengeance to the wound of the oppressed," he wrote.

### Creativity &amp;amp; memberships

Navoi built the "Ikhlosia" madrasah in Herat, the "Khalasiya" khanaka, the "Shifoiya" hospital, and the "Khusraviya" madrasah in Marv. Sources state that the number of structures built under his patronage exceeds 300 (Khatifi — 373), including more than twenty mosques, more than ten khanaks, sixteen bridges, and a water canal in Mashhad. More than seventy scribes and calligraphers worked in his library. Many writers such as Abdurahman Jami, Kamoliddin Behzod, Mirkhond, Khondamir, and Sultan Ali Mashhadi enjoyed his patronage.

### Literary heritage

In 1483-1485, Navoi first created a "Khamsa" in the Turkic language, consisting of five dastans; the previous "Khamsas" were in the Persian language. Navoi, the author of more than twenty works such as "Hazoyin ul-maoni," "Lison ut-tayr," "Mahbub ul-qulub," "Majolis un-nafois," "Mezon ul-avzon," and especially "Muhokamat ul-lugatayn," scientifically and literally substantiated that the Turkic language is not inferior to the Persian language. The poet expressed the idea of humanism in the following lines:

> Do not call me a man if you are a man,

> He has no sorrow for the sorrow of the people.

Navoi died in Herat on January 3, 1501. In 1991, the 550th anniversary of his birth was celebrated, and a majestic monument to him was erected in Tashkent.

## 7. Zahiriddin Muhammad Babur and the Baburid Empire

Zahiriddin Muhammad Babur (1483-1530) was born on February 14, 1483, in the city of Andijan, into the family of the ruler of Fergana, Umarshaykh Mirzo. In 1494, at the age of twelve, he succeeded his father as ruler of Fergana. Throughout his life, he attempted to restore the legacy of the Timurids, but failed to achieve this goal due to the superiority of Shaybanid forces.

| Year | Event |
|---|---|
| 1497 (November) | After a seven-month siege, he captured Samarkand |
| 1500 (autumn) | He occupied Samarkand for the second time |
| 1501 (April) | In the battle of Sarypul, he was defeated by Shaybani Khan |
| 1504 (September) | He established power in Kabul |
| 1511-1512 | With the help of Iran, he captured Samarkand for the third time but returned to Kabul after the Battle of Gijduvan |
| 1526 (April 21) | He defeated Ibrahim Lodi in the battle of Panipat; Founded the Baburid Empire (1526-1858) |
| 1530 (December 26) | He died in Agra; his ashes were interred in the "Bagi Babur" in Kabul |

Babur defeated the Delhi Sultanate in India and became the founder of the Baburid state. In his victory, firearms, the use of cannons, and the superiority of military tactics played an important role. Babur was not only a commander but also a great writer and scholar. "Baburname" is an encyclopedic work that covers the history, geography, peoples, and nature of Maveraunnahr, Khorasan, and India. He also wrote treatises on "Mubayyin" (tax and law issues), "Hatti Baburi" (simplified alphabet), aruz, and military affairs. The daughter of Gulbadanbegim is the author of the work "Humoyun-name." The English historian W. Hansen characterized the three hundred and thirty-two-year reign of the Baburid dynasty as the "crown" of Indian history. In Babur's poetry, human qualities are also celebrated:

> He who is faithful will find loyalty

> He who oppresses seeks punishment.

## 8. Social and economic life

### Farming &amp; Gardening

During the Timurid era, agriculture formed the basis of economic life. Amir Temur prohibited the construction of yurts near crops, new canals were dug around the Murghab oasis and Kabul, and canals were built on waterless lands. Wheat, rice, and cotton were grown; Since the time of Timur, flax has been sown. Parks such as Bogi Dilkusho, Bogi Behisht, and Bogi Baland have been established around Samarkand. The Spanish ambassador Clavijo, approaching the city, described the scene he had seen: so many orchards and vineyards that the city could be seen in the middle of a tall forest.

### Land ownership relations

The main forms of land ownership were state, private, waqf, and communal lands. Suyurgal is a land property provided for service and possessing tax and administrative-judicial immunity, which subsequently acquired a hereditary character. The Tarhan exempted the owners of the warrants from taxes, and their mistakes were forgiven up to nine times. The growth of Suyurgals and Tarkhans reduced treasury revenues and weakened central power, paving the way for fragmentation.

### Crafts and trade

Samarkand paper, the highest quality of which was called "sultani," became especially popular in craftsmanship. As Babur wrote, "The best paper in the world comes from Samarkand." Purple velvet, saqirlat, and brocade fabrics, as well as Bukhara's zandanachi fabric, were popular and were sold even in Novgorod and Germany. Pottery, jewelry, the manufacture of colored glass, and the production of weapons were also developed; Tashkent's "Kamoni Shoshiy" bows were especially valued

Amir Temur revived the Great Silk Road and built caravanserais along the roads. In a letter to King Charles VI of France, he wrote: "You will send your merchants to my kingdom... For the world will be made prosperous by merchants!" Silk and porcelain were brought from China, spices from India, and broadcloth and "farangi" fabric from Europe

### Tax system

The main taxes were kharaj, tagar, tamga, peshkash, and sovurin. In the "Tuzuk," it was required not to take goods from the subjects until the harvest was ripe, and to renounce violence when collecting taxes. Those who cultivated abandoned land were not taxed in the first year. In 1404, Amir Temur exempted the inhabitants of the empire from paying a three-year tax. However, in the next stage of the Timurid era, the tax burden was particularly worsened by wars.

## 9. Science, Literature, and Art

In the 15th century, Mawarannahr and Khorasan once again became the centers of enlightenment and culture in the East. After the death of Ulugh Beg, the cultural center moved to Herat. This period is often referred to as the "Temurid Renaissance" because science, literature, fine arts, and architecture reached a high level simultaneously.

### Literature

In Turkic literature, Lutfi (1366-1465, "The Flower and Nauryz"), Sakkaqi, Durbek ("Yusuf and Zulaikha"), Gadoi, and Atoi created works. Abdurahman Jami (1414-1492) occupied a leading position in Persian-Tajik literature; The friendship between Jomi and Navoi became a symbol of the unity of our peoples.

### Art &amp; Architecture

The Herat school of miniature art was led by Kamoliddin Behzod (1455-1537); In Europe, he was called the "Rafael of the East." Author of the portraits of Behzod Navoi and Husayn Bayqara. In calligraphy, Sultan Ali Mashhadi (1432-1513) elevated the Nasta'liq script to a high level. In the art of bookbinding, the warrak (paper maker), calligrapher, artist, muzahhib (gilt-maker), and sahhof (binder) worked together. In music, twelve maqoms were enriched with new melodies, and Jomi and Binai wrote treatises on music.

In architecture, the Registan ensemble was formed in Samarkand: the Ulugbek Madrasah, the Shahizinda Complex, and the Gur-Emir Mausoleum were built; The Gavharshadbegim complex was erected in Herat. These structures, being high examples of Eastern architecture, still inspire admiration among people around the world.

## Conclusion

After the death of Amir Temur, the kingdom was plunged into a whirlpool of struggle for the throne, but Shah Rukh Mirza ruled it stably for nearly forty years and established extensive diplomatic relations. Mirzo Ulugbek turned Samarkand into a center of world science; his observatory and "Zij" left an indelible mark on the history of astronomy. In the second half of the 15th century, the empire was divided into Khorasan and Maveraunnahr; Under the patronage of Sultan Husayn Bayqara and Alisher Navoi, Herat became the cultural capital of the Muslim East. The Suyurgal system, internecine wars, and the disunity of the rulers weakened the state, leading to its liquidation by the Shaybanids in 1507. The legacy of the Timurids was continued in India through Babur and the Baburids.

## Key concepts

- **Suyurgal** — land property provided for services, possessing tax and judicial immunity.
- Tarkhan is a privileged person exempt from taxes.
- **Kuragan** — "Khan's groom," a title given to grooms from the Chingizid dynasty.
- **Zij** — a collection of astronomical tables.
- Zullisonayn is a poet who writes in two languages.
- Khamsa is a cycle consisting of five dastans.
- **Book Art** — the art of making handwritten books.
- The Ulugh Beg Academy is a 15th-century scientific center in Samarkand.
